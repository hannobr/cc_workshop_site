The rail

.step-sheet, docked

The rail is not a second list. It is the page's own .step-sheet, the same markup that is the bottom-sheet dialog below 900px, docked as a 200px sidebar at 900px and up. It is the first child of .frame on every step page so it can own grid column 1. All of its type, colour and geometry live in workshop-nav.css. A page-local .step-sheet__* rule would restyle the real component, so this specimen only supplies the surrounding width and ground.

Row vocabulary: __mark bookend · item · is-done · is-current · __break · __group · __fold · __alt · __sum

The specimen stands with route A open (data-route-a="open"). On a live page those five rows are folded away until that route is picked.

Reading the rows: green tick in the minutes column is done, blue fill with the inset left bar is the current step, amber is the break. The break row is a link to break.html, the stop where the participant chooses how they build; it takes is-current there, in amber rather than blue. The two afternoon routes are lettered rows on the same track as the steps, so A and B line up in one column: .step-sheet__group above step 04 is a button, the door to the seven .step-sheet__fold rows under it; the .step-sheet__alt row under step 08 opens route-b.html, the afternoon that is not the guided steps, taking is-current in blue. They mirror the fork the run sheet on the index carries, and the fold follows the pick made there. data-route-a on the list holds it: closed, open, or here on one of the five, where the caret goes and the A turns blue because the step being read is inside that route. The closing .step-sheet__mark (Demos and wrap-up), the break row and both route rows are not .step-sheet__item rows and carry no data-step, so workshop-nav.js ignores them. Every item carries data-min. An estimated item renders the same number in a .step-sheet__d span; Build leaves both values blank because its plan determines the duration. .step-sheet__sum is written by the script from the current row; it is empty in markup. On a live page the hrefs are the real step files, and only one row is current. Below 900px these same rows grow to 44px tap targets inside the dialog, which is what the specimen shows on a phone.

Numbered blocks

.blocks · .blk · --now · --pass

A build step after the hero is one .blocks section holding exactly three .blk blocks, numbered 0, 1, 2: 0 why this step exists, 1 write the prompt yourself (blue), 2 pass check (green). The design rule the specimen carries: one heavy object per build step, and it is the brief card inside block 1 (until 2026-09-01 it was a prompt card; route A build steps carry no pasteable prompt any more). Blocks 0 and 2 stay light, a 1px rule on a soft tint. Never give block 0 or block 2 a dark fill, a thick border, or a numeral heavier than the brief; that is what makes the card the participant writes from win at a glance from across a room and beside a terminal.

Pass check

docs/intent.md is committed, and it names who is stuck and what they can do once this exists. Also: you can point at one line you changed in the prompt and say which of Claude's answers caused it.

Missed? Ask Claude who the file is about, and have it say so in the first sentence.

Passed? The intent file is what step 05 reads.

On a build step the pass check IS block 2. It is not an aside.pass-check and it carries no .pc-label. The .pass-check primitive survives for concept steps and is inventoried under Challenge steps.

.miss-cases · when more than one thing can go wrong

A .miss with one recovery move stays a sentence, and most of them should. When a step can miss in two or three distinguishable ways, the symptoms have to be findable: a participant reads this under time pressure, scanning for the line that matches what is on their screen, and a symptom buried mid-paragraph is not findable. Symptom in <b>, fix behind it, one case per row. A .miss with five cases is a step doing too much; fix the step, not the list. Live use: step-06.html, block 2.

2

Pass check

In the fresh session, ask whether the project works yet without naming the skill: it reports FAIL and names the criterion that failed.

Missed? Match what you saw:

  • Nothing ran? The description has to name the moment. Add the words you actually used, then restart.
  • Ran the command itself? That is the body asking rather than forcing. Say "use the verifier subagent", watch it comply, then note that you had to say it.
  • PASS already? Part of it already works, which is normal outside a workshop.

Passed? Sticker up.

The musts sit on white with an empty checkbox each; the where row and the tips sit on the panel grey, so the must-haves are the brightest thing in the card. Three rows, in the order the prompt gets written. .brief__row--where names the playbook stage and play the step interprets, with the link, and ends by printing the address itself as the link text, not the word "playbook": the participant has to type it into the prompt, and a link they can only click is a stall. .brief a wraps anywhere so the address never widens the card. .brief__row--must is what has to be in the prompt: the file at its fixed path under docs/, the commit, and only what the next step reads. Each row label ends in a .brief__sub line that prints the test sorting a line into that row: a must is what a later step or the pass check needs. Musts carry an empty ink checkbox, tips a dim dash; never restyle one to look like the other. Since 2026-09-03 every must carries its reason on a second line, .brief__why: what goes wrong, or which later step reads it, when the must is missing. It renders at 15px in --ink-2, so the must stays the line the participant checks against and the reason sits under it, read and never copied. A must with no why line is a finding. Tips keep their reason in their own sentence. No accent colour on the paper: the amber dot survives only in the dark head band. The row labels and the stage name are load-bearing and render in --ink, never --ink-3; the must label is uppercase and the heaviest mono weight, the heading of the checklist. Step IO

.step-io · one per page · last child of .hero on a build step · retired from live pages 2026-09-04, archive/two-route-fork/ only

The at-a-glance band: what this step reads, the one thing the participant decides, and what they leave with. It sits under the meta-chip and above the blocks, so the page answers "what am I doing here" before any prose runs. Live use: step-04.html through step-08.html.

The middle cell is the reason the component exists. This project's test for a build step is "what does the participant decide here, and does the pass check test that decision?", and before this band that answer only surfaced in block 2, under the prompt. Naming it in the hero is what lets the rest of the page cut its prose: the band states what happens, so block 0 only has to argue why. A step whose middle cell cannot be filled in as a phrase is a step whose decision has not been found yet, and it belongs folded into its neighbour rather than shipped.

Construction follows two ways, context pair, runs when and lands: CSS boxes holding real DOM text so it reflows, zooms and stacks, and no role="img" and no aria-label, either of which would make the cell text presentational. It parts company with those four in two ways. It is not a <figure> and has no figcaption, because the six labelled cells already read as the text alternative in order. The white panel is the live one in this system, and the live one here is the participant's own job.

Two ways

.two-ways · one per page · block 0 of a build step

The "instead of what?" figure, and block 0's answer to why a build step exists. Every step from 04 to 08 is a swap: there is a way this normally goes, and the step trades it for a different one. As prose that reads as an assertion the participant has to take on trust, because the alternative it argues against is never on the page. Put both on the page and the reader compares two concrete situations instead of believing one claim. Live use: step-08.html, block 0 (07 dropped its copy on 2026-09-03, the is/is-not caption carries that argument; 04 and 05 carry lands instead).

The two panels are deliberately not identical chrome, which is where this parts company with Context pair: there the two windows are two of the same thing, here they are the default you are leaving behind and the thing you do today. The left panel sits on --bg-2 and the right on --paper, so the white one is the live one. That tint carries the whole "you are here" job, which is why the component needs no accent colour. Each panel ends in a .two-ways__then line, and those two lines are the argument: what the usual way costs, and what this step buys. Write them as consequences, never as slogans. If a __then line could open a landing page, the figure has stopped teaching.

Context pair

.ctx-pair · one per page

Two context windows side by side, one arrow out and one arrow back. It exists to make a subagent has its own context window visible instead of asserted, so both boxes carry identical chrome (they are two of the same thing) and the asymmetry lives entirely in what each one holds. The argument it draws is the tool split, not the token saving: the side that reports the verdict has no Edit and no Write, so it cannot make its own verdict come out differently. That is why each window ends in a .ctx-pair__because line, and why those two lines are the only sentences in the figure. Live use: step-06.html, block 0.

Side by side above 560px of figure width, stacked below it

Never put a count in .ctx-pair__keep. "1,400 lines of output" is an invented number, it is false on a repo where nothing is built yet, and it invites arithmetic against the 1M window step 03 just showed. Say there is output; never say how much. No accent colour, either: green is PASS and blue is NOW in this system, block 0 sits directly above the blue .blk--now, and a build step gets one heavy object. Emphasis is weight, so the returning arrow is the darkest line in the figure. The figure carries no <p>: inside a .blk, .blk p is 0,1,1 and outranks every rule in the component. And it takes no role="img" and no aria-label: the panel text is real text in the accessibility tree, either attribute would make the whole subtree presentational, and the figcaption is the text alternative. Keep the caption free of left and right, which stop being true when it stacks.

Runs when

.runs-when · the companion to the context pair

What a step leaves in the repo, and what makes each piece actually run. Context pair answers "who runs the check and why would you believe it"; this answers the question the reader asks next, which is "and what makes any of it fire?" The second column is the whole figure. Read down it and the answers get stronger without the page claiming they do: nothing calls it, someone has to ask, nobody has to ask, Claude is not asked at all. Live use: step-06.html, block 0. The retired Conventions page carried it too, until 2026-09-03.

It is not a ladder and it takes no numbers. The rows are files in a folder. A numbered version of this idea shipped once into a page whose blocks are already numbered 0, 1, 2 and was cut for exactly that collision. The repo boundary is drawn, not described: the skill that belongs to no project sits outside the box in a .runs-when__away line, which is the whole personal versus project distinction without a sentence spent on it. A file that is real but is not today's work sits inside the box as .runs-when__row--later, on a dashed rule with a .runs-when__tag.

Spar

.spar · a round with Claude, turn by turn · retired from live pages 2026-09-04, archive/two-route-fork/ only

The build steps stopped printing prompts on 2026-09-01, and since 2026-09-03 the participant does not draft alone either. What a participant takes home is this round: set the scene for Claude (workshop, own idea, a loose version of the playbook with the link, the stage the card names), ask Claude to help write the prompt, answer its questions, hold each proposal against the card's must list, go round until every must is covered, then clear and paste the finished prompt. That is a sequence of who acts on what, which is what a figure is for. The prompt round is drawn once, in block 1 of step-04.html directly under the block's intro paragraph and above the /clear chip and the brief (the brief is taller than a screen, and a first-timer needs the shape of the round before the contents of the card), with id="spar". The class name and the id are from the sparring round this replaced; they stay so the CSS and the links from steps 05 to 08 stay put. Those steps carry a one-line .hint that links back here and adds one thing to check before the run; they never carry a second copy of the prompt round. Step 07 uses the primitive once more for a different conversation, its round two (since 2026-09-03): Claude shows the plan and offers to leave plan mode, the participant asks the playbook's three questions, pushes back, and approves only once the push-back is in the plan. Five turns again, Claude's two on --bg-2, the approval as the run turn.

Two columns: who, and what they do. Five turns: you set the scene, Claude asks and proposes, you hold it against the card, you go round again, you run. Claude's one turn sits on --bg-2 so it reads as the other voice. The last turn is the run and sits under a heavier rule: everything above that line is conversation and nothing has run yet. The things to tell Claude are a .spar__asks list in the first turn, with plain dash markers and no numbers, because the page's blocks are already 0, 1, 2. Claude's turn carries the worked example, .spar__eg: the first proposal's line, the must it left out, the line added, in the same mono box two ways uses for a thing someone typed. On step 04 the example is the must block 0's figure warns about and the pass check tests, so blocks 0, 1 and 2 point at the same line. The card is the checklist and is never pasted to Claude: that is what makes the round a round.
